# Which apps to integrate next: a ranking for small companies

Researched 2026-10-21. Scope: companies of roughly 5–200 people, non-technical, where the buyer is an owner or ops person. The question is which apps Mycel should integrate after Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 and Slack, which other agents are studying (they appear below for comparison only).

## How to read the evidence

- **No primary source ranks app use among 5–200-person non-technical companies specifically.** Every signal below is a proxy, and each has a bias:
  - **Zapier's and Make's app directories** (default "most popular" order, fetched 2026-10-21) are the closest proxy for Mycel's buyer: small teams that already automate "when X, do Y" between SaaS apps. Neither publishes the counts behind its order, so treat these as ordinal ranks only. Zapier's category pages give a popularity order within each category.
  - **Okta Businesses at Work** counts apps by number of Okta customers. Its "small" bucket means under 2,000 employees (71–72 apps per company), so it leans toward mid-market and tech companies. Use it only as a cross-check.
  - **Vendor figures** (10-K, 20-F, 8-K and ASX filings, plus vendors' own About pages) are counted differently from one vendor to the next: paying customers, organizations, users, or only customers above some spend level. Compare them only roughly.
  - **Productiv and BetterCloud** were not used. Their published data covers mid-market and enterprise SaaS spending, and I found no small-company breakdown.
- **Nango column:** taken from Nango's `packages/providers/providers.yaml` (last commit 2026-10-19).
  - "OAuth" means Nango handles the auth.
  - "webhooks" means the provider entry has a `webhook_routing_script`, so Nango can receive that app's webhooks and forward them.
  - "API key" or "basic" means the user would paste a credential, which works against Mycel's "OAuth only" rule.

## Scoring

Each app gets 1–5 on five criteria, combined with these weights:

| Criterion                                                                                                                                            | Weight |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| (a) Adoption among small companies                                                                                                                   | 30%    |
| (b) Repetitive, describable "when X, do Y, a person approves" work flowing through the app                                                           | 25%    |
| (c) API openness for a third party                                                                                                                   | 20%    |
| (d) Nango support                                                                                                                                    | 10%    |
| (e) Fit with Mycel's approve-before-it-leaves model: does the app have a native **pending form** (a draft or unsent state) for its outbound actions? | 15%    |

The scores are my judgement over the cited facts. They are not a measurement.

## Recommended shortlist for a full capability study

These are the top non-Google, non-Microsoft, non-Slack apps, picked to cover the categories where small-company work repeats most: sales, money, work tracking and support.

1. **HubSpot**: the highest score among non-suite apps. 306k customers and Zapier's #6 app. A free tier with full API access, OAuth and forwarded webhooks; lead and deal follow-up is the classic supervised workflow.
2. **QuickBooks Online** (study **Xero** alongside it as one "accounting" study): Zapier's #1 and #2 accounting apps. Both have native unsent and draft invoice states that fit the pending form. Watch Intuit's call metering, Xero's connection pricing and AI-training clause, and the fact that sending an invoice is probably `financial`.
3. **Pipedrive**: the SMB-native CRM with 100k+ companies. The most open API here (every plan, OAuth, webhooks v2), and deal nudges are highly repetitive.
4. **monday.com**: 250k+ paying customers and Zapier's #2 project-management app. Intake → item → status → reminder workflows, mostly `internal_reversible`, so they are safe to automate early.
5. **Asana**: a widely used task tool with OAuth and webhooks on every plan. Pairs with monday as the "work tracking" pattern. ClickUp and Trello can reuse the same catalog shape later.
6. **Notion**: in the top 10 on both Zapier and Make, with OAuth and forwarded webhooks. It is both an action target (trackers, notes) and a rich source of org context for the builder.
7. **Zendesk**: Zapier's #1 support app with 80k+ companies; ticket triage and reply drafting is very repetitive. Two catches: Zendesk must approve a global OAuth client, and the pending form would be an internal note. If that approval or that pending form is a blocker, study **Help Scout** instead (smaller, but it has native draft replies).
8. **Shopify** _(only if the target customers sell online)_: millions of merchants and Zapier's #1 e-commerce app. Draft orders are a perfect pending form, but the protected-customer-data review and GraphQL-only rule add up-front work.

Near misses:

- **Zoho CRM** (4.25): study it instead of Pipedrive if the target customers lean Zoho.
- **Airtable**: the Free plan's API limit of 1,000 calls/month cripples it.
- **Calendly**: largely visible already through Google Calendar.
- **DocuSign**: strong pending form, but low frequency.
- **Salesforce**: no API on small-business plans without a paid add-on.
